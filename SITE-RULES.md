# Site rules

## Purpose

- Every page is evidence of thinking; the line behind all of it is “Find what’s already true. Say it plainly.”

## Look

- Keep the shell quiet: white page, black ink, 1px lines, and no gradients, shadows, or blurs. Color enters through the work.
- Each project owns one color taken from something real in that project. Use it in at most three places: hover fill and transition, one page accent, and one homepage detail.
- Use flat colors only. Do not place long text on saturated color. Every text/background pair meets 4.5:1 contrast. Keep all colors as CSS variables.
- Use one typeface family with weights 300 and 400, sizes 14px, 18px, 24px, and one display size.

## Motion

- Only the project-block hover fill and open/close frame transition, and a 300ms opacity-only content fade-in are allowed.
- Other hover states are an underline or a 4px arrow shift. Reduced motion disables all motion.

## Behavior

- Every project has its own URL; the back arrow always works; no link goes nowhere; navigation works without JavaScript and by keyboard; tap targets are at least 44px.

## Honesty

- Label every project Real, Pilot, or Concept. Give every number a source and date; credit collaborators; get written permission for client work; do not leave placeholder text on a live page.

## Case studies

- Keep each case study to 350–500 words, at most six images, and one muted loop video.
- Use this order: header (label, title, one-sentence problem, status Real/Pilot/Concept); “Find what’s already true”; “Say it plainly”; “What changed”; “What it cost”; credits with a link to the next project.
- Keep sections to 90 words or fewer and paragraphs to four lines or fewer. Place every claim beside a number, quote, or artifact. Use “I” for my thinking and “we” for team decisions.
- Never use authentic, passionate, storytelling, synergy, or journey. Every image caption names the decision it shows; put the strongest proof in the first two screens.
